| 2026-10-23 | Plumbing and electricity | **Allowed on higher-tech, self-sustaining compounds, but only through real infrastructure that costs something.** Running water needs a source (well / spring / cistern), pressure (gravity tank or pump), pipe runs, fixtures and waste handling (outhouse → composting toilet → septic tank + leach field). Electricity needs generation (solar / micro-hydro / wind / generator + fuel), batteries, an inverter and wiring. Each costs materials, labour, know-how (someone with plumbing / electrical skill, or slower and riskier without) and upkeep (pump wear, battery ageing, frozen pipes in winter, fuel). Interiors show the tier: oil lamps, hand pump and outhouse at first; faucets, a water heater, a flush toilet and electric lights once a building is connected. |

| 2026-10-23 | Water + power model (provisional) | **Pipes and cables are drawn lines** (trenched by the family, costed per length) that join buildings into a water network and a power network. Sources: hand-dug well + hand pump (no pressure, water is carried), drilled well (contractor drilling; the pump needs power), rain cistern (fills from connected roofs by season), gravity water tower; waste via outhouse → septic tank + leach field. Power: solar array, battery + inverter shed, generator shed (burns fuel). Each building gets **Plumb it / Wire it** jobs (fixtures / wiring kit + labour; faster and safer with a mechanic) that change its interior (faucet instead of the hand pump, water heater, flush toilet with septic, ceiling lights, a fridge). A **daily balance**: solar by season, demand per connected building, battery state of charge, generator fuel, water supply vs. use, rain into cisterns. Upkeep: battery fade, pump failures (repairs), generator fuel. Shortages are logged (morale effects wait for needs). |

| 2026-10-24 | Underground (provisional) | **Bunkers are built room by room** (GDS §2.2 underground layer). A bunker starts at an entrance (a steel hatch with a ladder down a 5 m shaft, or a stairwell under a small headhouse); every other room must share at least one 3 m cell of wall with a room already planned, and gets a doorway through it. All rooms of a bunker share one floor level, the entrance's ground less ≈ 4.8 m; each room needs ≈ 0.9 m of earth over its roof slab and can't be dug deeper than ≈ 6 m. Rooms go under the yard, gardens and houses but not under the creek, wells or the septic field. Rooms: tunnel, buried 40 ft container (the cheap one), bunkroom (8), galley, storeroom, plant room (NBC filter, batteries, generator, 2,000 L tank), medical bay, comms & armory. Digging uses a hired excavator; the net earth dug out goes on a **spoil pile** that an earth berm draws on (60 % less shovel work); ditches add to the pile. While a room is built the terrain over it is an open pit (dug → walls poured → roof slab, waterproofed → backfilled). **Underground view** (U, or the Underground button; it comes on when you pick a bunker room): see-through ground, trees hidden, surface buildings ghosted, bunker rooms cut open. Walking down into a bunker, the escape tunnel to a hidden exit, and bunker water / power / air (the plant room feeding the rest) come next. |

| 2026-10-24 | Escape tunnel (user: "very securely locked from the inside … outsiders should NEVER be able to bust through from the exit side"; "a looooong tunnel"; "half a mile long or longer"; "dimly lit"; "hidden/buried"; "too conspicuous out there in an open field") | A line drawn from a free wall of a bunker room out to an exit (Underground menu → Escape tunnel), ≥ 60 m and with its exit ≥ 50 m from every building; it may **run on under the neighbors' land** (up to 360 m past the line, +40 h of work for that stretch), so routes of half a mile or more are possible. **Inside** (the user's photo): a whitewashed, board-formed concrete arch you walk upright in (2.2 m wide, 2.6 m to the crown), a wet concrete floor with a drain gutter, a cable run on hooks, and one caged warm bulb every 24 m: dim (only the four nearest bulbs cast light). Cut and cover with a hired excavator, poured in 6 m vault sections (trees stay; only the hatch spot is cleared). The floor leaves the bunker level and follows the land at ≤ 8 % so there is always ≥ 0.9 m of earth over the vault. **Egress only, absolutely:** `canEnter(run, 'outside')` is always false: no event, raid, tool or stat may ever open it from outside (threat systems must call `canEnter` and respect it). The bunker end is a blast door shut in its frame with two steel drop-bars on the room side; the exit is a chamber with a ladder up a concrete shaft to a steel lid with no handle, hinge or hasp outside (hinges, drop-bolts and locking wheel on the underside), its collar sunk below grade. **Hidden:** in the trees it is buried under soil, a thick layer of leaves, fallen branches, a fallen trunk and brush; in open ground it sits under a hand of turf, flush with the grass, so nothing shows. | 2026-10-24 | Bunker hardening (user: "lined with nuclear proof/fallout survival material, steel cage/faraday cage shield emp, etc. … upgrades that obviously cost more money") | Four upgrades, each in three tiers, ordered per room in the Hardening panel or with "Whole bunker"; the family fits them as retrofit jobs (sim/hardening.ts). **Fallout:** Basic: extra earth and a shielded hatch (PF 40) → Shelter: dense concrete and a baffled entry (PF 1,000) → Military: lead and steel lining (PF 10,000+). Small rooms take half the materials and time. Each tier is visible inside: copper strap, rubber door gaskets and a filtered entry box, mesh at the ceiling seams, deep concrete reveals, lead sheets with seams, the HEPA blower, red blast valves, a pressure gauge, green blast doors, I-beams and posts. | 2026-10-24 | Food and needs (user: "Calories + variety + spoilage"; "Injuries heal with care; death possible") | `sim/needs.ts`, run once a day. While the stores work the family eats from them (part of household costs). After that it eats from the stockpile: fresh food first (it spoils: 8 %/day in the open, 1.5 % in a root cellar), then freezer meat (thaws 30 %/day once the root cellar's freezer has no power), then canned, then bulk staples. Need: 2,400 kcal per adult, 1,700 per kid. **Grown food:** about a dozen hens give 3–8 eggs a day, more in summer; a kitchen garden peaks at about 2,500 kcal/day in August and September, better with a gardener; a hunter (skill 2+) sometimes gets a deer in the fall (about 40,000 kcal). **New materials:** freezer meat $65 / 10,000 kcal, a case of canned food $36 / 6,000 kcal, bulk staples $120 / 80,000 kcal, medical kit $150. A new game starts with two cases of canned food. **Each person has** health, morale, hunger (calories short, accumulated), radiation dose and wounds. Hunger and wounds cost health; a dose above about 1 Sv causes radiation sickness. Wounds heal faster with a medic, medical supplies (a kit lasts about ten wound-days) and a medical bay. Morale drifts toward a target raised by variety, a home and light, and lowered by hunger and a death. Low health or morale slows work. At 0 health a person dies (Cozy floors health at 5). |
| 2026-10-24 | Neighbors, arrivals, encounters (user: "4–6 households"; "Stance choice + vignette card"; "Threat-type matrix"; "Humanity track graded"; government "Help-or-hindrance roll") | `sim/encounters.ts`. **Neighbors:** 4–6 named households per seed (old farmer, young family, prepper, widow, ranch, loner), each with a disposition (−100 feud … 100 allies), size, a radio or not, and their own skill. | 2026-10-24 | Nuclear war (user: four warning tells, "Distant flashes + sky", "Distance-based" dash, "Managed shelter life", severity roll with a rare nuclear winter, dose per person with contaminated food and water, Faraday room protects what's inside) | `sim/nuclear.ts`. **Dose:** the dose rate at the compound follows R1 × t^-1.2 (the 7-10 rule). The HUD shows the outside dose rate (mSv/h), where everyone is sheltering and its PF, and the days until people can go back out. A "Replace the fried …" button appears on fried buildings. Save v13. |
