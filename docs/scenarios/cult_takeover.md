# Scenario: cult_takeover (the Covenant: a religious or ideological takeover)

Kind id: `cult_takeover` (new; brainstorm rank 3). A charismatic movement fills the vacuum, absorbs the neighbors and takes the local government. There are no monsters: the horror is persuasion, tithes, and a community that can say yes. Not about a real religion: the Covenant is a fictional movement. Conventions in `README.md`.

## 1. Real-world basis and analogs

| Analog | Facts | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Rajneeshpuram, Oregon, 1981 to 1985 | A commune of about 2,000 bought a 64,000-acre ranch; took over the town of Antelope's council; in 1984 bused in about 2,000 homeless people to win a county election and poisoned salad bars with salmonella (751 sick, no deaths) to sway the vote. | Local government can be captured by a motivated minority; the neighbors are the target. |
| Jonestown 1978 | Peoples Temple in Guyana: 918 died on Nov 18, 1978, about 300 of them children. | Isolation and a leader's control end in disaster; leaving is hard. |
| Waco, 1993 | 51-day siege of Mount Carmel; 76 died on April 19. | Outside force can harden a cult. |
| Munster 1534 to 1535 | Anabaptists took the city council; community of goods, polygamy; a 16-month siege and a bloody end. | A takeover can start with a fair election. |
| Taliban, Kabul 1996; Boko Haram 2014; ISIS in Raqqa and Mosul 2014 | Order imposed on a lawless country; food, courts and fear; the first years popular with some. | A harsh order beats chaos for some; the cult sells safety. |
| Aum Shinrikyo 1995; Heaven's Gate 1997 (39 deaths) | A doomsday cult attacking or dying. | The leader's apocalypse timeline. |
Figures are widely cited (Oregon records, Guyana investigation) and should be rechecked before display.

## 2. Warning signs (10 to 40 days, or "creep" before the world event)

A charismatic preacher gathers a following at the fairgrounds; free food and a van at the grocery for those who sign; a new church opens in the old feed store; neighbors begin giving away possessions; the Covenant's flag in windows; missing teenagers; a community meeting packed with strangers; a bus of new voters at the county clerk's office; the sheriff "retires" and his deputy is a member. Notice: neighbor cards show "attends the Gathering" (a tag), radio local churches, and the town council vote on the day card. When combined with a disaster (usually chained after lawless or famine) the Covenant arrives with food.

## 3. Phase timeline

| Phase | World | Compound |
|---|---|---|
| Hit moment | The Covenant takes the town council by vote; announces "the Order": food from the common store for members; the sheriff sworn in as "Guardian". Title: "The Gathering has come to the valley." | Visitors at the gate offering a blessing and bread. |
| First hour | A procession down the main street; loudspeakers at dusk. | |
| Day 1 | Two neighbors move into the Compound of the Covenant; their houses stand empty. | An invitation (a written, courteous card). |
| Week 1 | Free meals, medical help, work crews. Members offer "fellowship." Some neighbors join. | A "visitor" asks to see your stores to "help". |
| Month 1 | The Order sets rules: curfew at night, a tithe (10%) from all within the district; unbelievers pay 20%; markets only at the Common Hall. | A tithe collector. |
| Month 3 | A school, a court (the Elders' Court), a militia (the Shepherds). Defectors and dissenters "go on retreat" (detained). | A request to send your children to the school; a labor levy. |
| Month 6 | Expansion: the Covenant takes the next town; raids or "missions" at holdouts; food shortages hit outside; inside feels safe. | Pressure intensifies: join, pay, leave, or fight. |
| Year 1 | An internal crisis: the Prophet's date for the End arrives, schism; or the movement stabilizes into a theocratic county. | |
| Year 2+ | Collapse (a Jonestown-like end), stable theocracy, or crushed by outside force (a Waco-like assault). | The ending depends on the player's role. |

## 4. Effects

Tracks (variant `the_gathering` as a chain-on to a base disaster or on its own as a "soft" collapse): law C(0,20,0.5,null) (the Order replaces the law; the track means the legitimate law); stores C(10,40,0.6,[200,500]) for outsiders, but the Common Store is up; deliveries C(5,40,0.5,[200,500]); medical C(5,30,0.6,[200,500]) (the Covenant runs a clinic: a service the player can use at a price); banks C(20,40,0.4,[300,700]); grid and phone mostly up: the Covenant controls the town radio; fuel C(10,30,0.5,[200,500]).
Prices: the Common Hall sets prices: a "fellowship price" (x1) for members, x3 for outsiders; barter in labor or "devotion points" (hours of service); the tithe in kind.
Encounter mix: new `evangelist` (2 to 4 members, courteous, offer bread; one in four is a recruiter asking for a tour and a count), `tithe` (4 to 8 Shepherds with a ledger: an organized `requisition`-like encounter, `hard` temper, formal: "The Order asks a tenth."), `defector` (a refugee fleeing the Covenant, scared, fragmentary: "Don't tell them I came here. Please."), `inquisitor` (late; an Elder's court summons), `missionary raid` (late; 10 to 20 Shepherds, in the `savage` tier only in the `zealot` variant: they kill unbelievers who resist but spare children and take them in "for raising").
Hostile crews (the zealot raids) kill all adults who resist and take captives and children; merciful elders spare children and let families leave with what they can carry.
Health: good inside (a clinic) and poor outside; ideology-driven deaths late (refusal of treatment, the Prophet's fast), stress, defection trauma. Food: inside secure, outside scarce.

## 5. Factions

The Covenant (the Prophet, the Elders, the Shepherds militia, the Common Store), the Holdouts (neighbors resisting; a committee), the Remnant Sheriff, the Defectors, the Outside Authority (state Guard, arrives late if any).
Checkpoints: the Common Hall road gate, a Shepherd roadblock with a tithe ledger; the county line. Stances: pay the tithe, convert (a "sponsor" of a family; benefits), feign belief (a hidden Gray option with a discovery roll each Gathering), refuse, fight.
Requisitions: tithe (10% or 20% of stores every 30 days; hide a cache), labor levy (an adult 2 days a week), school (children 6 to 16 attend), a "gift" of the weapons (a collection). Conscription of youths into the Shepherds in month 6.
Collaboration vs resistance: Join (inside is safe and fed; humanity gain from belonging but a hidden "freedom" cost to your family's agency; the end report asks what you did to dissenters), Feign (spy for the holdouts; risk), Hold out (a committee of neighbors with a pact; raids), Escape (defectors network; help those who flee, +10 humanity, +raid risk), Destroy (assassinate the Prophet: a one-time heroic event with consequences; outside Authority arrives).
Humanity: giving a defector shelter +10; turning one in -20; paying the tithe -1 (a cost to your freedom, not a moral cost); letting your child join without asking -8; exposing a hidden family -25.

## 6. Regional variation

| Region | Notes |

| appalachia | Strong church tradition: revivalist spiral; snake-handling and tent revivals; kin pressure; the Covenant spreads through families. |

| plains | Church towns, grain; the Covenant controls the grain elevator. |

| new_england | Town meetings are the vulnerable point; skeptical culture; slower. |
| ozarks | Revivalism and militia; strong holdouts and strong recruits. |
| hill_country | Megachurches and ranch networks; the Covenant is a political machine. |
| rust_belt | Desperate towns where the Covenant feeds people; high recruitment. |

## 7. Radio and day cards

Radio: the Prophet's evening address (text lines: "A tenth to the Common Store, and no one shall hunger"); the town council vote tally; a list of "those who have joined"; a pirate holdout net; an outside bulletin: "Reports of detentions in the valley". Day cards: "Day 6. A girl from the church van waves at your fence. Her smile is too tidy." "Day 90. The Elders' Court has posted a list. Your neighbor's name is on it."

## 8. Decisions; rich vs poor

Rich: donate a lot to be a patron (the Order gives you immunity for 90 days; humanity cost), buy out the sheriff, hire outside security, legal action (while courts exist), leave early to another county, finance the holdouts. Money buys time. Poor: the Covenant's food and clinic are tempting (the "poor" are the main recruits): a no-cost safe path with a hidden cost; or the poor hold out with labor sharing.

## 9. What makes it fun

A social, psychological scenario: the enemy is nice. Players navigate persuasion, hold-outs and clandestine support rather than shooting. A gentle slow burn with a late crescendo. It is non-gory and non-repetitive, a change from raiders. Multiple endings (reform the Order from inside, tear it down, flee, convert).

## 10. Numbers and hook seam

Variants: `gathering` (benign, persuasive, weight 3), `zealot` (aggressive, raids unbelievers, weight 2), `prophet_date` (a doomsday timer: at a date the Prophet foretells (day 120 to 300), the Order takes extreme actions or collapses, weight 1). Tithe 10% members / 20% unbelievers every 30 days; recruiter visits every 10 days; neighbor conversion rate p 0.03 per day per neighbor with hardship above 0.5; the Order's militia strength 5 at month 1, 15 at month 6; discovery chance of a hidden cache 0.1 per tithe visit; feign discovery p 0.08 per Gathering.
Hook seam: `src/sim/disasters/cult_takeover.ts`; `onWarning` (creep tags), `onHit` (council capture), `daily` (conversion, tithe, court), `encounterWeights` with `ExtraEncounterKind` `evangelist`, `tithe`, `defector`, `inquisitor`; `priceMods` (member and outsider prices), `radioBulletins`, `oasisWeights` (humanity and law weights), `look` (lamps, procession), `medical` (clinic service). Neighbor disposition uses `neighborLife` with a `faith` field in `fx`. Can attach as a modifier to any collapse (`modifier: 'covenant'`) so it chains rather than replaces.

## 11. Chaining

Into: `lawless` (a vacuum, 35%), `global_famine` (the Covenant feeds, 30%), `unknown` (a sign), `pandemic` (a faith movement), `financial` (people give their savings). Out of: `civil_war` (the Order vs the holdouts, 25%), `lawless` (the Order collapses, 30%), `mass_exodus` (defectors flee, 20%).
