# Scenario: unknown (the unexplained event, four hidden truths)

Kind id: `unknown` (exists, pulpy; today half the neighbors vanish once and encounter weights shift). Redesign: one visible hit, **four hidden-truth variants** rolled at the hit and never named in play. Replays differ because the same warning signs, radio lines and day cards mean different things. Conventions in `README.md`.

## 1. Real-world basis and analogs

There is no real event; the basis is how real populations behave when the explanation is withheld.

| Analog | Figures and facts | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Orson Welles, War of the Worlds broadcast, Oct 30, 1938 | Panic accounts exaggerated by newspapers, but some listeners did flee and phone lines jammed. | Radio rumors outrun facts. |
| 1980 Mount St. Helens, 1986 Chernobyl evacuation of Pripyat (about 49,000 evacuated within 36 hours on April 27, 1986), the Hawaii false missile alert of Jan 13, 2018 (38 minutes) | Official silence and false alarms; people act on the first message. | Hit-moment confusion, delayed official truth. |
| Roanoke Colony 1590 ("Croatoan") | 115 people gone, one word carved. | The vanishing variant. |
| Havana syndrome, the 1947 Roswell story, the 2004 and 2015 Navy "tic-tac" sightings | Unexplained but documented; secrecy. | Government secrecy and conflicting stories. |
| Fiction: Dies the Fire (the Change), A Quiet Place, Leave the World Behind (2023), The 5th Wave | | Variants C, B, D. |
| Event after-effects: mass sociogenic illness, doomsday cults, the 1844 Great Disappointment (Millerites) | | Cults and waiting crowds. |

Sources are fiction and the documented sociology of panic (Quarantelli); no figures need checking beyond the Pripyat and Hawaii facts above, which are public record.

## 2. The shared surface (what the player sees before the hit, 3 to 10 days)

Same for all four: lights over the range three nights running; compasses point west; cattle standing in rings; a tone on the radio at 3 a.m.; a truck in the neighbor's drive with doors open; the sky hums. Plus **tells** that appear only in some variants (the player who writes them down on the day cards can guess):
- A: animals vanish too; clocks agree; no sound.
- B: dogs and wildlife go silent (no birds); a low pulse at night heard through the floor.
- C: static on all radio bands a day before; lighters fail once; flashlight batteries drain faster; an aurora over the wrong latitude.
- D: the same "lights" appear on the TV news from other regions with a time stamp offset by an hour; the tone repeats at an exact interval; a government spokesperson in a prerecorded video.

## 3. The four variants and their hidden truths

### A. The Vanishing ("they were taken")
Truth: about 40 to 55% of people within the region are removed in the light; they return, unchanged and confused but for some who are different, after 40 to 120 days, or never (fate). The sky quiets. Not hostile.
- Hit: half the valley gone; cars with doors open; a half-eaten supper. The family compound is intact (the player is spared by `family is home` roll; or one family member is gone, 25% if away).
- Tracks: grid C(0,0,0.6,[20,90]) (no operators), phone C(0,2,0.3,[20,90]), banks C(0,2,0.3,[30,120]), law C(0,3,0.3,[60,200]), stores C(0,5,0.4,[40,120]), deliveries C(0,5,0.3,[40,120]), medical C(0,3,0.3,[40,120]), fuel C(0,5,0.4,[40,120]). Neighbors: half the NPCs gone; their stores and animals abandoned (free salvage, with a moral choice: take or guard for their return).
- Encounters: scavengers 1.5x, looters 1.5x, `kin` rolls dramatically; wandering cattle and dogs; "the returning" in weeks 6 to 17: neighbors who return (a "kin" encounter with odd lines: "How long was I gone? It was only a minute.") and some return changed (gentle, will not eat meat; or aggressive) or bringing a strange object. Raiders are few in number early, because the gangs are gone too; but the orphaned and scared (children left alone) are many: take them in for +6 humanity each.
- Fun: a mystery, the moral weight of salvage, and the return as a mid-game twist. Fate `full`: everyone returns at day 60 with no memory, the world resumes with confusion and lawsuits; `never`: they never come back.

### B. The Quiet ("it hunts by sound")
Truth: creatures that came with the lights hunt by sound; they are fast, armored, blind; they killed about 30% of the region in the first night. Silence is survival.
- Hit: a night of screams, then very little sound at all; fires burn untended; half the neighbors dead in their houses.
- A noise meter: sounds the player makes (chopping, generator, gunfire, dogs barking, vehicles) add `noise` in the `fx` bag; above a threshold each night a hunter arrives and attacks (a `horde`-style encounter of 1 to 3 creatures, strength 90, kills sentries). Quiet builds: sand paths, a soundproofed basement or bunker, felted doors, a water-powered alarm light (the red-light signal), hand tools instead of power tools, bows and suppressed or no firearms, sign language.
- Tracks: grid C(0,3,0.4,[100,400]), phone C(0,1,0.1,null), law C(0,2,0.1,null), stores C(0,5,0.2,null), deliveries C(0,3,0.1,null), medical C(0,2,0.2,null), fuel C(0,3,0.2,null), banks C(0,2,0.1,null).
- Raiders: human raiders are few (they die fast) but are desperate and noisy (a gang that fires guns draws creatures onto itself and onto the compound: a deadly mixed raid). Humans who survive speak in whispers and gestures, text lines are short, in italics: "Shh. Please. Behind you."
- Fun: a stealth-and-sound design game inside the compound; every build has a noise stat. Fate `full`: creatures die off or leave after about 18 months (a hidden clock), `partial`: they move to the coast, `never`: permanent.

### C. The Change ("the rules are different")
Truth: a physical change: explosives (gunpowder), high-voltage electricity and internal combustion stop working in part or fully (the Dies the Fire rule). Hidden rule: it can be partial (1 shot in 5 fire; electricity fails above 12 volts) and may reverse.
- Hit: planes fall, cars stall on the highways, grid dead, firearms click; lighters and matches still work (low chemistry; an optional line).
- Tracks: grid C(0,0,0,null) except a "low-voltage" exception: batteries, small solar and wind 12V still work; fuel C(0,0,0,null) for engines; phone C(0,0,0,null); banks C(0,0,0,null); stores C(0,2,0.1,null); deliveries C(0,0,0,null); medical C(0,2,0.2,null); law C(2,10,0.1,null).
- Gameplay: every firearm is a club; bows, crossbows, swords and hand tools matter; horses and oxen, wood heat, hand pumps; a city of 1 million starves in weeks and the walkers arrive from the city (refugees 4x by week 3, hundreds); the encounter mix replaces guns with melee; raiders have bows and pitchforks; in Dies the Fire the warlord with medieval-recreation men rises (a `militia` of 20 by month 2). Hostile crews kill all but take captives and children; merciful crews are farmers.
- Regional: Appalachia, Ozarks, plains (farm skills and draft animals) do well; Rust Belt, desert, PNW cities suffer.
- Fate `full` (the Change reverses at about year 3 to 5), `partial` (guns work again at 25% reliability), `never`.

### D. The Static ("it was done by people")
Truth: the lights were a deliberate hoax: a foreign electronic and psychological operation (jamming, spoofed broadcasts, drones with lights) hiding a real grid and communications attack (the Leave the World Behind pattern). There are no aliens. Government secrecy covers an embarrassing failure. The true scenario reveals itself by day 30 as a hybrid of `cyberattack` and `invasion` soft.
- Hit: lights, the tone, then the grid and phone and TV fail in a patchwork; GPS gone.
- Tracks: grid C(0,2,0.2,[14,120]), phone C(0,0,0,[20,150]), banks C(0,1,0,[30,200]), fuel C(2,8,0.3,[30,150]), stores C(3,10,0.3,[30,150]), deliveries C(1,5,0.2,[30,150]), medical C(5,20,0.5,[40,160]), law C(10,30,0.5,[60,200]).
- Gameplay: a puzzle of rumor: false radio voices (aliens! the president!) appear on bands and a few true ones; the player learns which sources to trust (a ham net that cross-checks). Reward for verifying (Standing with the ham net), penalty for acting on a false order (a fake evacuation order empties the road and the compound is looted). Strangers are paranoid; people kill each other over a misunderstanding; humanity track tests the trust vs paranoia choice.
- Fate: `full` (services return in 3 to 5 months, the spin story is revealed), `partial`, `never`.

## 4. Common effects and encounter mixes

Shared: panic buying in the 10-day warning (priceFactor), rumor tags on the radio with a **reliability marker** (true, false, unknown) that only sometimes shows; churches and cults grow; some neighbors prepare for "the rapture" and abandon their farms (a salvage opportunity); a roadside prophet encounter (a `stranded` variant) with doom speech.
Raider types by variant: A (few; orphans), B (rare, noisy), C (many: cities walk out, melee), D (opportunists using the real chaos; fake "officials" with forged orders: an `impostor` card that tests papers).
Health: A none special; B injury from creatures (tear wounds; shock), C hunger and wound infection from melee; D stress.

## 5. Factions

A: the Returned (post day 40); B: the Hushed (quiet communities), the Loud (gangs that ignore the rules); C: the Bearkillers analog (a farm community), the Protective Association analog (a warlord with a medieval-themed force); D: the Ham Net, the "Officials" (impostors), the Citizens' Committee. Humanity consequences: A (taking in orphans, guarding abandoned goods) +, looting a neighbor's goods while they may return -10 later when they do; B (leaving a screaming stranger outside to avoid noise) -15 but survives; C (refusing hungry refugees) -8; D (trusting a liar and giving away your radio) 0 but harmful, executing a suspected impostor wrongly -25.

## 6. Regional variation

Appalachia and Ozarks: folklore and church networks; high belief, strong response to A and B; plains: the farm advantage in C, long sightlines in B; desert: dry nights show lights, strong hits in D (military bases); mountain_west: a high light show; pnw: big cities to the west make C devastating; new_england and north_woods: quiet communities adjust best to B; rust_belt: D and C worst (dense, dependent); hill_country: ranch culture; upper_midwest: balanced.

## 7. Radio and day cards

Radio: variant-specific bulletins appear from the hit but with wrong or hedged lines early on (A "mass disappearance, investigation underway"; B "avoid noise, sirens suspended"; C "all vehicles and weapons failing, stay home"; D "official statement: the lights were a weather phenomenon" then a contradicting one). The day-30 and day-90 bulletins reveal part of the truth. A hidden lore log ("the Notebook") stores clues for the end report.

## 8. Strategies, rich vs poor

A: salvage and storytelling (moral); B: quiet building (rich buy soundproofing and bunkers pre-hit if they read the early tells; poor use sand and cloth); C: low-tech stock (rich: pre-buy bows, a draft horse, hand tools, a hand pump and seed; poor: improvise) with a bonus to those already homesteading; D: information discipline (rich: a ham radio and a Faraday box; poor: ask the neighbor).

## 9. Fun

The mystery itself: players try to guess the variant from clues; each replay re-rolls with the same sky. Variants are different games in the same compound: moral salvage, stealth, medieval, intrigue.

## 10. Numbers and hook seam

`variants`: weights A 3, B 2, C 2, D 3 (D lowest cost to implement as it reuses `cyberattack`/`invasion` machinery). Warn 3 to 10 days in all; hidden truth `fx.truth` in {'vanishing','quiet','change','static'}. Noise threshold for B: 12 points per night; sources: gunshot 8, chainsaw 6, generator 3, shout 2, dogs 2; decay 6 a day; hunters attack when noise > 12 with p 0.5 per night. C partial rule: gunfire works with p 0.2; electricity above 12 V is dead.
Hook seam: `src/sim/disasters/unknown.ts` with `onHit` (roll truth, remove 40 to 55% of NPC neighbors in A, kill 30% in B), `daily` (noise tick, returns timer, hunters), `encounterWeights` per variant, `priceMods`, `radioBulletins` (variant tables with reliability markers), `buildingDamage` none except B creatures' attack damage on a loud compound, `oasisWeights`, `look` (the purple glow, with a color shift per truth revealed later). C needs `weapons.ts`, `vehicles.ts`, `utilities.ts` read `fx.truth === 'change'` (a small additional file `src/sim/change.ts`); B needs `noise.ts` (new, pure).

## 11. Chaining

A chains into `lawless` (15%) when orphans and looters grow, `global_famine` if farms are untended (10%); B chains to nothing but can start `lawless` among the Loud (20%); C chains to `global_famine` (40%, cities starve) and `mass_exodus` (50%); D chains into `cyberattack`/`invasion` soft follow-ups (30%) and `lawless` (35%).
